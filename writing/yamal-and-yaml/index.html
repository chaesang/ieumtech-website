Yamal and YAML

A World Cup memory from the Google cafeteria — and a footballer whose name reads like YAML.

There are a few times I’m freshly struck that Google is a global company, and World Cup season was one of them. People gathered from all over the world work mixed together on one campus, and in California especially the diversity was treated not as something to hide but as an asset — so come the World Cup, a few conference rooms and cafeterias would naturally broadcast whichever match fit the time zone. It was probably 2014, the Brazil World Cup, close enough in time zone to sneak a look around working hours.

Even in small numbers, the clusters sitting together in Argentina’s light-blue stripes or Brazil’s yellow shirts were an enviable sight. We’d dig out the T-shirts we’d bought back in our Red Devils days — Korea’s supporters — and little Korean gatherings would form, with the positive side effect of strangers getting to know and introduce one another over a match. Come to think of it, not quite to the degree of 2002, but at the 2010 World Cup we cheered fairly organized (?), while in 2014 Korea didn’t do well, so I remember less of it.

One scene that stayed with me a long while was Spain versus the Netherlands, held in a fairly big cafeteria. Googlers in orange and in navy stripes sat split to either side of the room, tension thick, and passing by on the way to a nearby meeting I caught, live, the famous flying Dutchman — van Persie’s diving header — and stood there transfixed, watching to the end.

A 5-1 rout for the record books. So overwhelming that even the winning side couldn’t cheer freely — a grim mood. Then again, that tournament had Germany smash the host, Brazil, 7-1 in the semifinal, so even this game got buried… decisive proof that a sliding header is cooler than a scissor kick.

The Spain that collapsed so badly back then beat France yesterday and climbed to the final again. Spain’s rising star, Yamal, is good. I lean more toward the old names — Ronaldo, Messi, Modrić, Ochoa — but this name resembles YAML, used in the industry, so my eye catches on it once more, and I root for him a little harder.

Just as camel gets said kamul or kamel, they tell me YAML is pronounced yammel, yammul — and at first I dutifully, ignorantly, called it Y-A-M-L. On the hard-to-hear Fox broadcast, every time they shout “Yamal, Yamal,” I flinch. Looks like there’s one more game to watch, and I’ll root once more.
