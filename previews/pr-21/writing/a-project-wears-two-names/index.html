A Project Wears Two Names

Cousteau, then Calypso, then App Indexing — how a Google search project got its inside name and its name for the world.

Naming a team or a project is always a big, hard thing. Sometimes you go flat and functional — Sales 1, Dev 2 — and sometimes, because the people up the chain lose track, you pin the manager’s name on it and call it so-and-so’s team. Naming a project, a product, the efforts inside it, carries the same difficulty. The bigger and older the company, the more likely any decent name is already taken, or was taken once. When you do stumble onto a word nobody else is using, the moment it gets big enough to go outside you bring in experts; before that, introducing it means fielding a running series of so what is that, and why are you the one using that word. In a small company you just do it. In a big one, respecting the order already in place — finding your slot inside it — matters enormously. I wish someone had taught me this earlier.

Below is the four-year stretch I remember most fondly and most proudly at Google — the project and team I lived with — told through the names it wore.

Cousteau

On the main campus at US headquarters, there were busts of great figures from science, and one of them gives us the first name. In Korean it’s Kuseuto; his full name was Jacques-Yves Cousteau, and I only learned of him late, having grown up on a different set of famous names in Korea. He was the one they called the deep-sea explorer — after the Second World War he sailed the unknown ocean and became famous above all for exploring what lay beneath it. Anyone raised in the US had probably heard of him once or twice, which is why a bust of him stood on campus. The search team sat nearby, so I passed him often on walks after lunch.

At the time the search team was building a demo and pitching a vision: that Google Search would reach into the information locked inside apps — an unknown world — and extend its territory there. The deep-sea-explorer image landed well and pulled a bit of interest from leadership. But a person’s name is hard to carry as a project name, and that French spelling made it harder. Still, the scenario was a good one — Google Search conquered the web, that was taking the land; now we go to sea — and it carried us into naming the next effort.

Calypso

After some discussion the project took the name of Cousteau’s famous ship: Calypso. A rather plain-looking research vessel, as the photos show. But the name also happens to belong to a goddess going back to Greek and Roman myth, and after she appeared — a little frightening, but a goddess — in the Pirates of the Caribbean films, it stuck easily in people’s mouths, and the project took hold.

Understood as the mothership for exploring the deep, it got us called the “Calypso Team” inside search, and we sent our first effort into the world under that name. For three years after, on one of search’s front lines, fifty of us had a good run alongside a lot of other teams — limited-edition team T-shirts with the name on them, a global workshop for the members, all of it a good memory. And then, as if it were Calypso’s own fate, three years on the project and team were dissolved and drifted off over the horizon.

App Indexing

Inside search, Calypso was a reasonably famous project — but you couldn’t send that name into the world as it was, so when it went out, leadership named it “App Indexing.” That process — choosing a street name, a market name — is one the experts join. And with it we made our first debut on behalf of the search team, at an Android release and at Google I/O.

We landed on indexing to mean this: rather than the user first, we’d tell the content owners that if they let Google reach inside the apps they built, we’d bring them along in Google Search’s scenarios. Back then there was even a small argument over whether to say app or eopl — and by small I mean it was Korea, alone in the world, saying eopl. Today it’s two worlds, Android apps and iOS apps, but at the time Chrome apps and web apps still demanded their own attention.

A fun memory alongside this: we decided to buy the .app domain, and I went to the domain auction in person. That was more the Firebase team’s strategy than search’s, so I played a supporting part, and afterward came back to the web — building deep links into Android and the iPhone by default, among other efforts. Fairly unique work you’d be hard-pressed to try anywhere else.

ps. other names. Even after Calypso went out into the world as App Indexing, all sorts of features and experiments inside that scope were planned and pushed under various names. Some were literal — “Calypso everywhere” — but the implicit theme was always the sea. The ones I remember offhand: Davy Jones, Sealand, Seaworld. The Pirates of the Caribbean films had left their mark, and naturally the team loved that series.
